## Top 10 recommended

Order prioritizes the manuscript’s scientific problem and complementary collaboration roles, not university prestige. Huberman’s ranking exception is explicit. Each entry has a rationale and a proposed initial discussion.

1. **Nicola Marzari — University of Cambridge**

   Two 2026 PRL papers connect analytical viscous-heat equations, boundary conditions and experimentally accessible nondiffusive transport. [1](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/g9dx-hjyn) [2](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/3s96-7ghm)

   **Proposed discussion:** Ask for a focused check of how the resonance singularity and boundary-loss invariance map onto hydrodynamic heat theory.

2. **Youssef M. Marzouk — Massachusetts Institute of Technology**

   Recent optimal-design and derivative-informed inference work directly addresses what experiments can identify. [1](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/acta-numerica/article/optimal-experimental-design-formulations-and-computations/38BBD0DC1A0386FDF306B6C0167DF7D9) [1](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v27/25-0858.html)

   **Proposed discussion:** Ask about a sensor/time-design formulation and a rigorous distinction between structural null directions and finite-noise CRLB corridors.

3. **David G. Cahill — University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign**

   The 2024 probe-deflection study explicitly changes sensitivity to through-plane conductance, making the experimental-design connection concrete. [1](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/apl/article-abstract/124/4/042201/3050684/Probe-beam-deflection-technique-with-liquid?redirectedFrom=fulltext) [2](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/thermal-conductivity-of-polyurethane-thin-films/)

   **Proposed discussion:** Ask which additional observable would best distinguish relaxation from boundary/interface losses in a feasible experiment.

4. **Masahiro Nomura — The University of Tokyo**

   A recent graphite experiment demonstrates geometry-sensitive hydrodynamic phonon transport, providing a physical testing route. [2](https://mdr.nims.go.jp/datasets/fbc84fc5-1f97-4b6a-9529-3cd28f71f91c) [1](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.35848/1882-0786/ae45ec)

   **Proposed discussion:** Ask about realistic specimens, boundary scattering and whether the present slab model transfers to a measurable hydrodynamic regime.

5. **Samuel C. Huberman — McGill University**

   **Borderline institution:** the 2024–2025 second-sound papers offer a particularly useful critical check on phenomenological interpretations. [1](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-648X/ae0dd6) [2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38482073)

   **Proposed discussion:** Ask whether a proposed GK signature could be reproduced without hydrodynamics, and which microscopic observables distinguish the alternatives.

6. **Li Shi — The University of Texas at Austin**

   Recent graphene hydrodynamics research and high-conductivity materials expertise link constitutive assumptions to real material physics. [1](https://sites.utexas.edu/lishi/publications/) [1](https://sites.utexas.edu/lishi/publications/)

   **Proposed discussion:** Ask about scattering regimes and specimen selection that make separate GK relaxation/nonlocal parameters physically meaningful.

7. **Felix Hofmann — University of Oxford**

   Current transient-grating expertise and 2024–2025 thermal-property studies offer a practical metrology perspective. [1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1359645425006159?via%3Dihub) [1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1044580324005254)

   **Proposed discussion:** Ask how microstructure, surface condition and specimen damage should enter the nuisance-parameter and uncertainty analysis.

8. **Bingyang Cao — Tsinghua University**

   Current boundary-scattering simulations and multiscale thermal design match the numerical validation needs. [1](https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.110.024302) [1](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/jap/article/138/18/180901/3371748/Thermal-design-automation-TDA-for-multiscale)

   **Proposed discussion:** Ask for an independent transport-model benchmark of the heat-loss boundary assumptions and the usable continuum parameter range.

9. **Zhiting Tian — Cornell University**

   Two recent TTG/thermoreflectance studies directly measure anisotropic and interfacial thermal transport. [1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-61334-8) [2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40270317/)

   **Proposed discussion:** Ask whether complementary spatial/frequency measurements can test the predicted information loss without merely improving a singular fit.

10. **Andrew M. Stuart — California Institute of Technology**

   Recent inverse-inference and ensemble-estimation papers supply a rigorous mathematical complement to the heat specialists. [1](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/acta-numerica/article/ensemble-kalman-methods-a-meanfield-perspective/94C9B874BBD4F11B8D36DD42D9F01BC7) [1](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6420/ad847b)

   **Proposed discussion:** Ask about local-versus-global identifiability and posterior geometry near resonance; a proper prior must not be mistaken for new information in the data.
